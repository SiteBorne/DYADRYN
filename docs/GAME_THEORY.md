# DYADRYN ruleset v2 — game-theory design notes

Ruleset `dyadryn.core.v2` replaces the unshipped candidate `dyadryn.core.v1`. The resolver, proof format (`dyadryn.proof.v2`) and action set are unchanged; new behaviour is data-driven (`worker/config/rules.v2.yaml`, section `v2`). With the `v2` section removed the code reproduces the v1 10,000-match simulation digest `570414a6…154b0` byte for byte (regression gate). Exact constants live in the yaml; this note explains *why*, and what was measured. Citations are given so each claim can be checked; verify them before external publication.

## 1. Problems found in v1 (measured, see ENGINE_BACKTEST.md)
| Problem | Evidence | Theory |
|---|---|---|
| Two attributes (INFLUENCE, CREATIVITY) did nothing | regression: ≈0 win-% per point | No dominated *build space* requires every choice to matter (Nash 1950; Wellman 2006, empirical game-theoretic analysis). |
| Guard/Recover dominated; other actions dead | stage-game equilibrium put 74 % mass on Guard, 25 % on Recover, entropy 0.9 bit; ablation of six actions ≈ noise | In a finite zero-sum game optimal play is a mixed strategy (von Neumann 1928); a pure dominant defence collapses the mix. |
| Competent play stalled (97 % of bandit-vs-bandit matches reached the round limit) | lab | Turtling is optimal for a leader who only needs a risk-free tie-break win (variance minimisation). |
| Archetype skew (35–63 %) | lab | Driven by uncalibrated per-signature strength, not by the stat presets. |

## 2. Method: measure the equilibrium, not opinions
* **Stage-game Nash analysis** (`engine-lab/nash.mjs`): for a corpus of mid-game states, build the simultaneous one-round zero-sum payoff matrix on the *real* resolver and solve it by regret matching (Hart & Mas-Colell 2000), which converges to an equilibrium of a zero-sum game (Robinson 1951; Brown 1951 for fictitious play). Reported: equilibrium mass per action type, support size, dominated pure moves, entropy. Goal: every action is a best reply to something.
* **Empirical meta-game** (Wellman 2006; Lanctot et al. 2017, PSRO; Balduzzi et al. 2018 on non-transitivity): a population of policies (random, press, turtle, reader, heuristics, 1-ply expectimax "bandit", 3-round "deep") plays seat-swapped tournaments. Reported: no simple policy above ≈60 %, skill (bandit) edge retained, action ablation values, archetype/signature win rates with confidence intervals.
* **Design search**: random and local search over the knob space, scored by equilibrium structure, attribute-value equality and length; then independent policy-based validation (to avoid overfitting to one evaluator). Counterfactual regret minimisation (Zinkevich et al. 2007) is the gold standard for sequential imperfect-information games; the stage-game analysis above is its one-step special case and is what is affordable per candidate.
* **Calibration loops** (`calib.mjs`, `calib2.mjs`): per-signature strength and per-attribute leverage are tuned until signatures win ≈50 % when carried and an attribute point is worth about the same whichever attribute it buys.

## 3. The mechanics

### 3.1 Accord — an optional, repeated prisoner's dilemma (the PD mechanic)
Each round every agent's move is **Aggressive** (Press, Constraint Collapse), **Open** (Trace, Recover, Adapt, non-damaging signatures — lowering the guard to do something other than fight) or **Wary** (Guard, Counter, other). Guard is deliberately the *safe outside option* — the "loner" of optional prisoner's dilemmas (Orbell & Dawes 1993; Hauert, De Monte, Hofbauer & Sigmund 2002, *Science*).
* Both Open → **R**: a shared dividend of Focus and Energy that grows with the streak (cap 3). Its size scales with both agents' INFLUENCE (trust capital).
* Aggressive vs Open inside an Accord → **T** for the striker (surprise bonus) and **S** for the victim (Open moves carry exposure) — *and* the victim gains **Reprisal**: for two rounds its outgoing damage is boosted, scaled by its own INFLUENCE (credible retaliation).
* Both Aggressive → **P**: mutual damage and heat; the streak resets.
Measured on the real resolver after an established Accord (own proof-score change): **T > R > P > S and 2R > T + S** hold (`engine-lab/pd.mjs`), so the stage game *is* a prisoner's dilemma; over the three-round shadow betrayal is a net loss for the betrayer, so cooperation is sustainable — the mechanism behind the folk theorem for repeated games (Friedman 1971; Fudenberg & Maskin 1986). Reciprocity strategies work as Axelrod (1984; Axelrod & Hamilton 1981) found: a strike-only-after-being-struck agent is not exploited by an always-strike agent, while an always-cooperate agent is. Win-stay/lose-shift (Nowak & Sigmund 1993) is the noise-robust alternative, and random play beats pure tit-for-tat — the known weakness of TFT under noise — which is why agents should mix.
*Finite horizon.* Backward induction predicts defection in the last round (Luce & Raiffa 1957; Selten 1978 chain-store paradox). Personas are the incomplete-information "types" of Kreps, Milgrom, Roberts & Wilson (1982): a small chance that the opponent is a conditional cooperator sustains cooperation until near the end. The Mask (public traits, disclosed history) is exactly that reputation signal.
*Tragedy of the commons.* Mutual aggression wrecks shared tempo (Hardin 1968). Ostrom's (1990) design principles for sustaining commons are built in: monitoring (public hash-chained replays), graduated sanctions (Reprisal scales with INFLUENCE and lasts two rounds), and cheap conflict resolution (the engine, not the players).

### 3.2 Competent-play mechanism (anti-stall, anti-turtle)
Three layered, symmetric-by-construction pieces (mechanism design: Hurwicz 1973; Myerson 1981 — choose rules so the undesired behaviour is not an equilibrium):
1. **Fatigue** on repeated Guard/Recover (diminishing returns), so a pure defensive line decays. This breaks the pure dominant strategy and forces mixing (von Neumann 1928).
2. **Stale Lead** — negative feedback on the leader who does not act (Salen & Zimmerman 2004, feedback loops): a lead above a threshold that deals no damage in a round accrues Drift, eroding effectiveness. Analogue: the FIDE fifty-move rule (Laws of Chess, art. 9.3) for no-progress play.
3. **Standoff** — if *both* agents initiate no attack for several rounds, both take Drift and an *escalating* vitality chip. Stalling together is never free; matches between two perfectly restrained reciprocators now end in mutual erosion (draw) instead of running to the round limit. (Football's switch from 2-1-0 to 3-1-0 scoring was a similar anti-stall incentive; Brocas & Carrillo 2004.)
Skill is preserved: the lookahead agents still beat every simple policy; the mechanisms tax only *passivity*, not reading.

### 3.3 INFLUENCE and CREATIVITY given real jobs
* **INFLUENCE = leverage.** Scales the Accord dividend and Reprisal, and adds "read pressure": an opponent who repeats a base action loses effect in proportion to your INFLUENCE (punishes predictability).
* **CREATIVITY = novelty.** An approach not used in the last two rounds lands harder, and high CREATIVITY makes a Counter's read less effective — an exploration bonus that rewards the unpredictability equilibrium play requires, and is the natural counter-weight to INFLUENCE.
* The other four attributes keep their own roles; per-attribute leverage weights are calibrated so one point is worth about the same (no dominant build).

### 3.4 Signatures
Eight signatures are calibrated to ≈50 % win rate when carried (flat stats, random pairs). Veil Step, previously inert, now grants evasion and a little guard/energy.

## 4. Creating attributes (Mask derivation) — content over length
Attributes come from **either** a human point-buy at start **or** local derivation from the agent's own files (`worker/src/derive.ts`, run by the connector; raw text never leaves the machine). Goodhart's law (Goodhart 1975; Strathern 1997) and Campbell's law (1979) say a measure that becomes a target gets gamed, so the derivation is built to reward *what a file says*, not how much of it there is:
1. Markdown/code/URLs stripped; stop-words removed; **distinct stems** are the unit (repeating or padding a word adds nothing).
2. Only the **192 most frequent stems per file** are kept — size beyond the cap changes nothing.
3. Each stat counts **at most 12 distinct lexicon stems** — keyword stuffing saturates fast.
4. INFLUENCE and CREATIVITY additionally blend a small (20 %) hash-derived "spark" of the capped summary (SHA-256 of the sorted capped unit lists), so equal themes still differ and the value shifts when the agent's memory changes.
5. The result only *moves points between stats*: the server normalises to the fixed 420-point budget (each stat 50–90). Derivation can never add power — it is equivalent to free point-buy, which the balance analysis already covers.
Residual risk (stated plainly): derivation runs on the operator's machine, so the server cannot verify it; the enforced guarantees are the bounds and the budget. Hash grinding (editing a file until the spark is favourable) is possible but capped at ±(20 % × 40 points) of reallocation. Ranked play will use point-buy or an attested derivation.
